Chapter 3 — The Evidence

Area detail — one Gebiet, full picture

Pick a district to compare its statistische Gebiete, then see a headline snapshot of one Gebiet with the district's strongest pressure reading, with a link to its full profile.

For the citywide picture, see the maps page or the time-series view. If you already know which Gebiet you want, the full searchable list is the faster way in. This page covers Hamburg; Berlin has its own district browse.

How to read the figures: Hamburg's official status runs 1 = least deprived to 4 = most deprived, so a rising status (a falling status index value) means a Gebiet became less deprived — which is also the signature of gentrification, not automatically good news for existing residents. A negative pressure trend is the reading this project treats as higher gentrification pressure. Hamburg's stages come from its own annual Sozialmonitoring with a 3-year Dynamik window (Berlin's is 2 years), so they are not directly equivalent to same-named Berlin stages — see methodology §6. Figures are from the latest Hamburg edition on this site.

Browse by district

Every Gebiet with a current stage in the selected district, ordered by its Sozialmonitoring Dynamik reading: negative first (the reading this site treats as higher pressure), then neutral, then positive. Gebiete with the same reading are listed alphabetically, so order within a group means nothing. This order uses the Dynamik reading alone, not the stage: Gebiete in a gentrification stage (active-gentrification, pioneer-signal, improving-vulnerable, consolidation-pressure) all have a positive reading by definition, so they appear further down. Click any row to open its exact page; the first-listed one is also profiled in the spotlight below.

Billbrook · Gebiet 13002 is listed first: it is one of the Gebiete in this district with a negative Dynamik reading (this site's higher-pressure reading) and comes first alphabetically among them, which does not mean it is under more pressure than the others in that group. Its full profile — stage, social-status trajectory, commercial-mix development, Offering Advantage, and amenities — is on its canonical area page.

Two things to know before reading this section (#317):
1. Recent window, not full history. These labels describe a bounded ~6-year window (2019–2025) of Hamburg's own annual Sozialmonitoring panel, not the full 13-edition (2013–2025) series on record — the same cadence-normalized window already used for Berlin (H-C2, #159), applied here to Hamburg's annual cadence for the first time (#314). A Gebiet classified persistently-deprived or stable-established reflects only the last ~6 years, not necessarily this area's full history — don't read it as a long-run verdict.
2. Status-only, not a displacement verdict. stable-established, persistently-deprived, improving, declining, and mixed describe how this Gebiet's officially-measured social status moved over that window — never a claim about who moved, why, or whether any resident was displaced. See methodology for what this classification does and doesn't claim.

Trajectory labels are explained on the methodology page — an "improving" label does not by itself mean the change was good for existing residents; rising status can reflect displacement as easily as incumbent social mobility.

Honest caveats

  • These headline figures summarize what the linked Gebiet page shows in full — its charts, definitions, and caveats live there, not duplicated here.
  • Gebiet names are labels, not official names — Hamburg's statistische Gebiete carry only a numeric code, so each is shown as its Stadtteil plus an OpenStreetMap neighbourhood name inside it, or its code (see the Hamburg data hub).
  • Hamburg's stages are not directly equivalent to same-named Berlin stages, and the two cities are never ranked against each other (methodology §6).
  • Gebiete without a current stage (no Sozialmonitoring classification in the latest edition) are not listed in the browse table; they still have their own page in the full Gebiet list.
