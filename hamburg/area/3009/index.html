Social status & trajectory

How to read this section: Hamburg's official status runs 1 = least deprived to 4 = most deprived, so a status that falls means the Gebiet became less deprived (its status rose) — which is also the signature of gentrification, not automatically good news for existing residents. A negative pressure trend is the reading this project treats as higher gentrification pressure. Figures come from Hamburg's own annual Sozialmonitoring — see the methodology & data sources page, §6 for how it differs from Berlin's.
Two things to know before reading this section (#317):
1. Recent window, not full history. These labels describe a bounded ~6-year window (2019–2025) of Hamburg's own annual Sozialmonitoring panel, not the full 13-edition (2013–2025) series on record — the same cadence-normalized window already used for Berlin (H-C2, #159), applied here to Hamburg's annual cadence for the first time (#314). A Gebiet classified persistently-deprived or stable-established reflects only the last ~6 years, not necessarily this area's full history — don't read it as a long-run verdict.
2. Status-only, not a displacement verdict. stable-established, persistently-deprived, improving, declining, and mixed describe how this Gebiet's officially-measured social status moved over that window — never a claim about who moved, why, or whether any resident was displaced. See methodology for what this classification does and doesn't claim.

Commercial mix & Offering Advantage

How its commercial mix has developed

Shops, cafés and other businesses tend to follow — not lead — social change (see methodology for the theory). This shows how the mix of mapped places here has evolved.

The most common kind of mapped place here in 2026 is Clothing; across Neustadt it's Sights, and across Hamburg as a whole it's Bench.

These are OpenStreetMap-derived counts. Mapper coverage grew fastest in Hamburg's earliest years (the citywide coverage curve stabilizes only around ~2014–2015, see the Hamburg POI map), so early-year growth can reflect new mappers catching up rather than real commercial change. OSM coverage is also not spatially neutral: poorer or peripheral areas tend to be less thoroughly mapped (Haklay 2010), so a low count can be a coverage gap rather than a real absence.

Offering Advantage profile

Offering Advantage (OA) compares each POI domain's share of this Gebiet's mapped places (shops, cafés, and other points of interest) to that domain's share across Hamburg as a whole — a compositional read on the local place mix, not a count, and not a value judgment: being over-represented in a domain doesn't mean an area is "better" or "worse," only that its commercial mix is more specialised in that direction than the city as a whole. The chart below shows each domain as a percentage above or below Hamburg's citywide average share for that domain — e.g. "+30%" means this domain makes up about 30% more of the local mix here than it does citywide on average; a negative percentage means the opposite, under-representation, shown the same way. The baseline is Hamburg's own, never pooled with Berlin's. For Hamburg, OA is commercial-side context only: Hamburg's published stage above comes from its own Sozialmonitoring, not from OA (see methodology §6). See the POI & Offering Advantage map to explore this across all of Hamburg.

Honest caveats

  • This Gebiet's name is a label, not an official designation (#307, #352). Hamburg's own statistisches-Gebiet source has no name field, so the name above is this Gebiet's Stadtteil plus either an OpenStreetMap place=neighbourhood/quarter name whose point lies inside the Gebiet (41 of 943 Gebiete) or, where there is none, the Gebiet's code. An OSM name marks a point inside this Gebiet; the neighbourhood it names may be larger or smaller than the Gebiet.
  • A falling status means the area became less deprived (its status rose) — which is also the signature of gentrification, not automatically good news for existing residents.
  • Hamburg's stage is not directly equivalent to a same-named Berlin stage — Hamburg's Dynamik window is 3 years, Berlin's 2 (methodology §6, point 1 and 4).
  • The trajectory labels describe a recent ~6-year (2019–2025) window, not this area's full 13-edition history, and are a status-only classification, never a displacement verdict (see the disclosure above that section).
  • Offering Advantage is descriptive, not causal, and multi-signed — over- or under-representation in a domain is a mix/specialization signal, never a standalone claim about gentrification. Domains flagged † above rest on fewer than 5 mapped places here and should be read cautiously.
  • Some Berlin sections are not shown for Hamburg — the written portrait, the status-over-time chart, within-group dominance, people & structure, and land value & rent — each with its reason in its own section above. See the Hamburg data hub for the full inventory and methodology & data sources §6 for how Hamburg's data differs from Berlin's.
