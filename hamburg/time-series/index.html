Time series — how Hamburg has moved

Rather than making you pick one of Hamburg's ~940 statistische Gebiete from a dropdown, this page zooms out: how Hamburg's Gebiete have moved over the recent Sozialmonitoring editions, and which of them moved the most.

To inspect any single area, use the district browser on the area detail page. This page covers Hamburg; Berlin has its own time series page, never combined with this one.

How to read status: Hamburg's official status scale runs 1 = least deprived to 4 = most deprived, so a negative change means a Gebiet became less deprived (its official status rose). "Rose" is not automatically good news for existing residents — rising status is also the signature of gentrification, and can reflect displacement as easily as incumbent social mobility. Hamburg's figures come from its own annual Sozialmonitoring and are never pooled or ranked against Berlin's (see methodology §6).
Two things to know before reading this section (#317):
1. Recent window, not full history. These labels describe a bounded ~6-year window (2019–2025) of Hamburg's own annual Sozialmonitoring panel, not the full 13-edition (2013–2025) series on record — the same cadence-normalized window already used for Berlin (H-C2, #159), applied here to Hamburg's annual cadence for the first time (#314). A Gebiet classified persistently-deprived or stable-established reflects only the last ~6 years, not necessarily its full history — don't read it as a long-run verdict.
2. Status-only, not a displacement verdict. stable-established, persistently-deprived, improving, declining, and mixed describe how each Gebiet's officially-measured social status moved over that window — never a claim about who moved, why, or whether any resident was displaced. See methodology for what this classification does and doesn't claim.

Hamburg, citywide: social status over time

Not published for Hamburg: A citywide status-over-time line (Berlin's page opens with one). Berlin's line reads fct_gentrification_change, which is kept Berlin-only until Hamburg has its own sign-off for it (the Hamburg admission sign-off's scope-guard condition 4). Hamburg's 13 annual editions are not drawn as a long-run series anywhere on this site yet: the Hamburg trajectory sign-off (#314) asks for any long-run Hamburg view to be a separately labelled product, not built from the 2019–2025 labels below. The current stage of every Gebiet is on Hamburg's map.

Honest caveats

  • "Rose" is not automatically good news for existing residents — rising status is also the signature of gentrification, and can reflect displacement as easily as incumbent social mobility.
  • The trajectory labels and movers describe a recent ~6-year window (2019–2025), not each Gebiet's full 13-edition history, and are a status-only classification, never a displacement verdict (see the disclosure at the top of this page).
  • No citywide long-run status line for Hamburg — see the note in that section for why.
  • Hamburg and Berlin are never ranked against each other — each city's status scale is read within its own official classification (methodology §6).
