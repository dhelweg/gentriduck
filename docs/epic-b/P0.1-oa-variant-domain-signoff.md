## 1. Construct validity — does the nested LQ still measure "offering advantage" in the theory sense?

### 1.1 The three-level parent-relative LQ is faithful to the thesis and to retail-succession theory

I traced the construct against `71_oa.sql` and `70_oa_helper.sql` and confirm the geo-DS §11.1
reading is exact, including the subtle nesting: **categories and types both normalize against their
parent *domain*, not the grand total and not the immediate parent category** (every `c_*` and `t_*`
column in `71_oa.sql` divides by `d_<domain>_stock`, e.g. `t_restaurant_italiener_stock /
d_gastronomie_stock`; the domain level divides by the all-domains total). The geo-DS formula
(`OA(t,a) = (t_a / D_a) / (t_city / D_city)`) is the correct operationalization.

From a domain standpoint this is a **location quotient of the local offering *mix*** — over/under-
representation of a POI category relative to the city-wide compositional norm. That is precisely the
quantity the retail-/commercial-gentrification literature treats as the observable "offering" side of
neighborhood change: the invasion-succession of the commercial tenant mix (Dangschat's invasion-
succession model applied to the retail landscape, not only the resident population), and the
"boutiquing" / amenity-upgrading signature described by Zukin (2009, *Naked City*) and
Lees/Slater/Wyly (2008, *Gentrification*, ch. on commercial gentrification). A Kiez shifting toward a
café/bar/gallery-heavy mix reads as high OA on those types **before** the social-status outcome
catches up — which is exactly the lead role OA is asked to play in H1–H3c. **The construct is
theoretically sound as a *predictor* (lead) of commercial change; it does not conflate cause and
outcome**, because the socio-economic status it is regressed against (EWR composite / MSS) is a
distinct, separately-sourced lagged outcome. This preserves the lead-lag separation that is the core
of the 2018 hypothesis and of my domain remit.

### 1.2 The parent-relative base is a feature, not a distortion of thesis intent

The task asks whether normalizing to city-wide weighted totals per level distorts what the thesis
intended. It does not, on two grounds:

1. **Denominator invariance (geo-DS §11.1).** The mass-conservation proof shows the city-wide
   *weighted* total per level equals the *hard* city total per level, so the OA denominator is
   identical across variants; the kernel only smooths the local numerator. There is therefore **no
   denominator-side theoretical drift** introduced by moving OA onto the weighted stocks — the "=1 is
   the city-average offering" interpretation is preserved intact.
2. **Parent-relative nesting sharpens the signal.** Normalizing a type against its parent *domain*
   (e.g. "of this Kiez's gastronomy, how café-skewed is it") isolates a cleaner retail-succession
   contrast than diluting every type against all POIs would. This is faithful to `71_oa.sql` and is
   theoretically preferable — it separates *composition-within-function* (which food/retail form is
   over-offered) from raw commercial density (which confounds with centrality). Good.

### 1.3 Two construct caveats the downstream build must carry (not blockers for the spike)

- **OA is a multi-signed bundle, not a monotone "gentrification score."** The faithful Run 1 keeps
  **all** types, including several whose sign relative to gentrification is null or *inverted*:
  `d_leerstand` (vacancy) is a classic **disinvestment / rent-gap** marker (Smith 1979/1987, rent-gap
  theory) — high vacancy-OA signals the *pre-reinvestment* trough, the opposite pole from amenity-OA;
  `d_religion`, `c_beerdigung` (funeral services), `t_pommesbude`, parts of `d_public_service` are
  incumbent-serving or sign-neutral. Faithfully reproducing the thesis means these enter as separate
  predictors and the regression sorts out signs — correct for Run 1 — but **no reader may sum raw OA
  columns into a single "how gentrified" index**, and per-type sign must be respected in
  interpretation and in any composite. This is exactly what the deferred Workstream-2 causal-tier
  curation (issue #170/#171) exists to handle; it must not be short-circuited. Captured as **D-2**.
- **Compositional small-denominator instability.** Because the LQ is scale-free, a low-POI PLR
  (peripheral, green, or purely residential) with a handful of POIs produces volatile shares and
  therefore volatile OA — a single POI can swing a type's local share. This is a known LQ weakness for
  thinly-populated areal units and it interacts with the leakage defect (§3 below). Captured as **D-3**.

**Verdict on Point 1:** construct-valid and faithful to thesis intent; no theoretical distortion from
the weighted-denominator move. Two interpretation caveats (D-2, D-3) bind downstream, not the spike.

## 2. Bandwidth — is a ~1 km catchment the right neighborhood scale for the Berlin gentrification reading?

**Yes, 1000 m is defensible as the headline; 500 m is too narrow; 1500 m is acceptable only as the
sweep's upper bound.** The reasoning from Berlin urban form (my lane, distinct from the geo-DS's
retail-gravitation citations):

- **The lived unit of Berlin neighborhood change is the *Kiez*.** Commercial gentrification in Berlin
  is read at the Kiez / Straßenzug scale — the transformation of a neighborhood's commercial-street
  offering mix (the Neukölln/Reuterkiez, Kreuzberg/Wrangelkiez, Prenzlauer Berg cases in the Holm 2010
  and Döring-Ulbricht literatures). A Kiez is on the order of several hundred metres to ~1 km across.
  A **500 m** catchment (~6-minute walk) captures roughly a single block-cluster / immediate walkable
  radius — finer than the scale at which residents actually experience "the offering" and finer than
  the scale at which retail succession is legible. **1000 m** (~12–15-minute walk) corresponds to a
  Kiez-plus-adjacent-commercial-street — the scale at which a resident's amenity environment and the
  succession signal are meaningfully constituted. This matches, on independent grounds, the geo-DS's
  retail-gravitation argument.
- **A compositional-metric reason *not* to over-widen.** Because OA is a share-relative-to-city
  quantity, widening the catchment pulls the local mix toward the city mean, so OA → 1 and the local
  retail-succession contrast is **washed out**. The very signal the metric exists to detect attenuates
  monotonically with bandwidth. This is a domain-grounded argument for keeping the headline at the
  neighborhood scale (1000 m) rather than going wider, and it *reinforces* the geo-DS recommendation.
- **1500 m does begin to overshoot the neighborhood reading.** At ~1.5 km (~18–20-minute walk) the
  catchment in dense inner Berlin spans **multiple Kieze and can cross social-area boundaries** (e.g.
  bridging a gentrified and a not-yet-gentrified Kiez, or crossing the historic East/West seam), which
  blends distinct social milieus and blurs the neighborhood-level contrast. It is appropriate and
  useful **as the upper end of a sensitivity sweep** (it stress-tests scale-robustness), but it should
  **not** be read as a neighborhood-level result and should not be a candidate headline. I endorse the
  {500, 1000, 1500} m sweep with the **1000 m headline** on these grounds.
- **On the thesis's unbounded catchment (geo-DS risk #3).** The 2018 OA used an effectively unbounded
  inverse-distance spread. From a domain standpoint an unbounded catchment is theoretically *too* wide
  — it maximally washes the local signal toward the city mean. So the revival's finite ~1 km catchment
  is not merely a faithful-backtest compromise; it is **theoretically preferable** to the thesis's own
  choice. The Run 1 divergence from the golden that this produces is therefore a *benign, improvement-
  leaning* methodological divergence to document under the Epic B directional-revival framing, not a
  defect. This strengthens geo-DS risk #3.

One limitation for the methodology page (not a blocker): Berlin amenity consumption is **transit-
structured** (U-/S-Bahn), so real catchments are anisotropic ribbons along transit corridors, not
isotropic circles. An isotropic Gaussian is a defensible first-order approximation; the G2 page should
name this as a known simplification. Noted under **D-1** framing, no action required for the spike.

**Verdict on Point 2:** 1000 m headline is neighborhood-scale-appropriate for Berlin; 500 m too
narrow; 1500 m sweep-only. Endorsed.

## 3. The leakage defect — domain-theory implications for cross-city / cross-time comparability

The geo-DS identified that at finite bandwidth, POIs beyond the bandwidth of *every* PLR
representative point (in large low-density PLRs — Tempelhofer Feld, Grunewald, Flughafensee) get
`total_weight = 0` → silently dropped, breaking mass conservation and distorting the OA base there.
From the domain angle this matters **more than its direct footprint suggests**, and I endorse C-1 as
blocking, with two additions:

1. **Direct cross-sectional impact is modest but the affected PLRs are not random.** The PLRs that
   leak are the large, low-density, often green/villa/allotment units (a park, a forest-villa area, a
   lakeside). These carry near-zero commercial-gentrification signal and few POIs, so the *direct*
   distortion largely hits PLRs that are off the gentrification map anyway — which is why this is a
   condition on the build, not a fatal flaw. But it is **systematic, not noise**: it biases against a
   *structurally defined* class of PLRs (large/peripheral/green), so any statement like "peripheral
   PLRs show no offering advantage" would be partly an artefact of the drop, not a finding.

2. **The real damage is to comparability — the foundation of the whole project's claims.** This is
   where the defect becomes a genuine domain-theory concern:
   - **Cross-time (H2/H3 change-on-change).** The 2021 LOR reform re-cut the PLR geometry, and the
     representative-point set differs across snapshots. If leakage incidence changes between years
     because the areal units changed, it injects a **spurious OA *change*** in the affected PLRs that
     an unguarded pipeline would feed straight into the lead-lag *change* analysis (H3a–c) — a data
     artefact masquerading as retail dynamics. Change-on-change is the most fragile part of the
     lead-lag design and the least tolerant of silent geometry-dependent mass loss.
   - **Cross-city (ADR-0005 city-agnostic core).** A metric whose completeness depends on the
     areal-unit *size distribution* is not portable: a city with a different PLR-size profile
     (Hamburg's Statistische Gebiete, or a future city) would leak differently, so cross-city OA
     comparisons would confound "different offering advantage" with "different unit geometry." A
     gentrification indicator that is not invariant to the areal partition cannot underpin the
     multi-city statistics site the project is aiming at.

   The leakage guard (fall-back to the POI's hard home PLR at weight 1) is therefore not merely
   spatial hygiene — it **protects the temporal stability and cross-city portability that every
   downstream gentrification claim rests on.** Assigning an isolated POI to its home PLR at weight 1
   is also the **domain-correct default**: a POI physically located in Grunewald *is* an offering of
   Grunewald, and weight-1-to-home exactly matches the hard-variant assumption. I endorse C-1 without
   reservation and rate it **blocking for OA-A.2 on comparability grounds, not only mass-conservation
   grounds.**

Residual note: even with the guard, large peripheral PLRs receive a de-facto hard-count while dense
PLRs are smoothed — a mild method heterogeneity by PLR type. Given these PLRs are near-zero-signal and
low-POI, this is an acceptable trade for restored mass conservation; it need not block anything, but
should be named in the G2 methodology limitations.

**Verdict on Point 3:** the defect has a real domain implication (systematic bias against large/
peripheral PLRs and, more importantly, a threat to cross-time and cross-city comparability). C-1 is
correctly scoped and correctly blocking.

## 4. Assessment of geo-DS conditions C-1 … C-5 + added domain conditions

The geo-DS conditions are the right ones and I adopt them. Domain-side endorsements and one
strengthening each:

- **C-1 (leakage guard + invariance test) — ENDORSED, blocking OA-A.2.** Strengthened: block it on
  **comparability** grounds (cross-time H3 and cross-city ADR-0005), not only mass conservation (§3).
- **C-2 (category-differential OSM completeness / tag-schema drift caveat on `prev_oa_*`) — STRONGLY
  ENDORSED.** This is the domain-critical one for the lead-lag reading: OSM tag maturation over
  2014–2020 (e.g. `amenity=cafe` maturing earlier than other tags) can manufacture a *spurious rise*
  in a lagged offering signal that is a data artefact, not real retail succession. A `prev_oa_*` lead
  effect driven by tag maturation would be a **false positive gentrification lead** — the exact
  cause/outcome-integrity failure my gate exists to catch. Do not read an attenuated *or* an inflated
  lagged-OA effect at face value without this caveat. Binds OA-A.4 / C.1 and the G2 page.
- **C-3 (OA-P0.2 ADR records the construct) — ENDORSED.** Addition: the ADR must **also** record (i)
  the per-type sign heterogeneity (vacancy/rent-gap vs amenity — D-2) so the construct is not read as
  a monotone gentrification score, and (ii) that faithful Run 1 intentionally includes non-causal /
  sign-ambiguous types with curation deferred to Workstream 2.
- **C-4 (flag OA as bandwidth-sensitive on G2 if rankings are fragile) — ENDORSED.** Addition:
  bandwidth-fragility is itself a **substantive finding**, not just a caveat — if the offering signal
  appears only at one scale, that reveals the spatial grain of the succession process and should be
  reported as such.
- **C-5 (domain-expert PASS) — SATISFIED by this document.**

Added domain conditions:

- **D-1 [Required — ethics/framing; binds G2 #-methodology page and O2 whitepaper #82].** OA is a
  **descriptive** indicator of commercial change consistent with *early* gentrification — it is **not**
  a causal displacement predictor and **must not** be presented as a targeting or "up-and-coming Kiez"
  signal. An over/under-representation retail map is precisely the artefact that can be misused to
  *accelerate* displacement (speculative targeting of Kieze reading high amenity-OA). Public framing
  must: (a) state descriptive-vs-causal explicitly; (b) foreground the power asymmetry that the *same*
  rising-OA signal is read protectively by residents/planners and extractively by investors (rent-gap
  logic, Smith); (c) name the isotropic-catchment and OSM-completeness simplifications. Inherit the
  #155 public-framing precedent.
- **D-2 [Required — interpretation; binds OA-A.4 #168, B.1/B.2 #170/#171, C.1 #174].** OA is a
  multi-signed bundle. No summing of raw OA columns into a single gentrification score; per-type sign
  (amenity vs vacancy/rent-gap vs incumbent-serving) must be respected. The causal-tier curation
  (Workstream 2) is the mechanism and must not be bypassed in Run 1 interpretation.
- **D-3 [Advisory — publication hygiene; G2].** The compositional LQ is unstable in low-POI PLRs.
  Consider a minimum-POI-base flag (or suppression) for OA in thinly-mapped PLRs before any per-PLR
  public display, so single-POI swings are not surfaced as neighborhood "advantage."

## 5. Verdict

The OA revival's three-level parent-relative location quotient faithfully operationalizes the 2018
thesis construct and is theoretically sound as a *lead* marker of commercial / retail succession in
the invasion-succession and rent-gap traditions; the weighted-denominator move introduces no
theoretical distortion (denominator invariance); the 1000 m headline catchment is defensible at the
Berlin Kiez scale with 500 m too narrow and 1500 m appropriate only as a sweep bound; and the mass-
leakage defect is correctly identified with real cross-time and cross-city comparability implications
that make the C-1 guard properly blocking. The spike's method decisions are docs-only and carry no
theoretical problem of their own; all binding conditions fall on the downstream build, analysis, and
publication tickets.

Geo-DS conditions C-1 … C-5 are adopted; domain conditions D-1 (ethics/framing), D-2 (multi-signed
interpretation), D-3 (low-POI hygiene) are added and bind the same downstream tickets.
